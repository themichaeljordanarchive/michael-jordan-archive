Archive Standards

Scope & Methodology

The Michael Jordan Archive documents and verifies game-worn memorabilia through primary-source evidence, repeatable comparative analysis, and stated research standards. Where an item cannot be confirmed, uncertainty is identified directly rather than filled by speculation.

Core Standard
Evidence First

Entries reflect confirmed visual evidence, supported identification, or clearly stated uncertainty rather than assumption.

Section One

Scope

  • Chicago Bulls game-worn jerseys documented by season and style.
  • Game-used sneakers and select related equipment when evidence allows.
  • Publicly surfaced examples through sale, exhibition, or published documentation.
  • Documented use cases where no garment has publicly surfaced, explicitly noted as such.
Section Two

Method

  • Comparative image analysis across multiple moments within the same game.
  • Wear-pattern continuity review, including repairs, marks, stitching, and material behavior.
  • Construction and tagging review where imagery or the object itself permits.
  • Cross-referenced game dates, opponents, uniform timelines, and contextual source material.
Section Three

Primary Sources

Research draws from a private archive of original photography and negatives, period publications, and broadcast footage, supplemented by additional contemporaneous sources when available.

When a source is incomplete, uncertain, or cannot be independently verified, that limitation is noted directly in the entry rather than concealed through stronger language.

Section Four

Population Research

The archive maintains an evolving season-by-season population record for known Michael Jordan game-worn jerseys. This includes tracking publicly surfaced examples, documenting sale or exhibition details when available, and recording privately verified examples when ownership requests non-disclosure.

Confirmed population totals may include privately held examples not publicly disclosed. Public examples reflect items that have surfaced through sale, exhibition, or documented publication.

This archive does not speculate. Entries reflect confirmed evidence, stated uncertainty, or work in progress.

Section Five

Sneaker Research

Sneaker identification within the archive follows a layered approach centered on observable wear and manufacturing characteristics specific to player-issued footwear.

Primary confirmation is established through direct visual correspondence to game imagery, including pair-specific wear patterns, material behavior, and structural details that remain consistent across documented use.

When visual confirmation is not possible, identification is supported through production coding, model chronology, and contextual research. In these cases, evidence is presented as supported identification rather than definitive photo-matching, while still confirming the pair’s manufacture and intended use for Michael Jordan.

Section Six
Artifact Attribution Standard

MJA Attribution Framework

A five-parameter evidentiary classification system used to evaluate the strength and completeness of artifact attribution research.

Each artifact is assessed independently based on the quality, quantity, and nature of the available evidence. Classifications provide transparency around how an attribution was reached.

Section Seven

Research Note

The Jersey Index is an ongoing documentation project. Entries are based on confirmed visual evidence, supported identification, or stated uncertainty.

When a garment has not surfaced publicly, it is documented through observed use and contextual research rather than speculation.

Section Eight

Acknowledgments

The registry reflects collaborative efforts across the collecting community. Appreciation is extended to private collectors and contributors who have shared documentation, imagery, and research in support of accurate population reporting.

Certain privately held examples are included at the request of ownership and remain undisclosed.
