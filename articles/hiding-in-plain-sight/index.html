Hiding in Plain Sight

A study of museum-held Michael Jordan game-worn jerseys and the role of photographic identification in revealing their true place within the historical record.

Some of the most important artifacts from Michael Jordan’s career have never actually been hidden.

They have existed in plain view—preserved, at one time or another, within museums, displayed within institutional collections, and presented as representative objects of basketball history. Yet in many cases, the precise use of these garments has remained undefined.

Through detailed photographic comparison, several of these jerseys can now be more clearly understood—not simply as symbolic artifacts, but as garments tied directly to specific moments on the court.

It begins in Chapel Hill, 1982.

Preserved behind glass within the University of North Carolina basketball museum is the jersey associated with the 1982 NCAA Championship— "The Shot", the moment that first introduced Michael Jordan to a national audience.

The garment has been displayed in several settings over the years, this one being the most recent. Identified simply as a North Carolina jersey from his collegiate career.

For decades, this jersey has existed in plain view.

Yet its significance extends beyond that general identification.

Through photographic comparison, supported by archival material assembled through the Michael Jordan Archive, the jersey can be directly tied to the championship game itself.

Construction characteristics align with game imagery from the defining possession, with additional reference imagery drawn from non-public archival sources confirming the garment’s use.

A representative comparison from the Archive is shown here, illustrating the alignment between game imagery and the surviving artifact.

What has long been presented as a representative artifact can instead be understood as the exact garment worn during one of the most consequential moments in basketball history.

This distinction materially reframes how early career artifacts are understood within the broader historical record. What appears to be a representative example, displayed for context or illustration, may in fact serve as a direct physical link to a defining moment — one that has remained visible, yet only partially understood.

More than a single identification, the discovery points to something broader — that historically important garments can remain publicly visible for decades while their full significance goes unrecognized.

This entry represents the opening case within a broader research study examining museum-held and institutionally preserved Michael Jordan jerseys.

Additional examples extending this pattern will be presented in a subsequent entry.
