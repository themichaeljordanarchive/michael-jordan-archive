The Nuances of the Jordan Jersey

A closer look at Chicago Bulls uniform inventory practices and the unexpected seasonal overlap revealed through photographic evidence.

During the course of documenting the 1989–90 Chicago Bulls season, a subtle but fascinating pattern began to emerge within the photographic record. What initially appeared to be routine game imagery soon revealed something more unusual: multiple players wearing jerseys manufactured for entirely different seasons.

The discovery began with a photo session taken following an April 1990 Bulls game. Within the same series of photographs, three Chicago players — Michael Jordan, Scottie Pippen, and Bill Cartwright — are all seen wearing home white uniforms. Closer examination of the tagging on each garment reveals that none of the jerseys originated from the same production year.

Scottie Pippen appears wearing a jersey tagged for the 1986–87 season, identifiable by the absence of a year identifier above the familiar 3" Length +2 Sand-Knit tagging. Michael Jordan, standing beside him in the same shoot, wears a jersey tagged for the 1988–89 season. Meanwhile Bill Cartwright, photographed during that same session, is confirmed through photomatching to a jersey produced for the 1987–88 season.

April 1990 Bulls photo session showing Scottie Pippen, Michael Jordan, and Bill Cartwright wearing jerseys produced for different seasons.

This evidence suggests that by the late 1980s the Bulls’ equipment room was not operating with strictly season-specific uniform sets. Instead, team inventory appears to have included usable stock from prior production runs that remained in circulation for multiple years.

Additional confirmation of this practice appears within the road red uniforms documented during the 1989–90 season. Two distinct road jerseys worn by Michael Jordan that year — each photomatched across extended game stretches — originate from earlier manufacturing runs. One jersey carries tagging consistent with the 1986–87 season, while the other corresponds to a 1987–88 tagged production.

Two road jerseys worn extensively by Michael Jordan during the 1989–90 season, originating from earlier Sand-Knit production years.

Together these examples illustrate a nuance often overlooked in the hobby: Chicago Bulls uniforms from this era cannot always be interpreted strictly by their tagging alone. While year identifiers provide valuable context, photographic comparison and wear analysis remain essential for determining actual on-court usage.

For collectors and researchers alike, this overlap of manufacturing runs offers a deeper understanding of how NBA uniform inventories functioned during the late 1980s — and serves as a reminder that the story of a game-worn jersey often extends beyond the season printed on its tag.
